# Positioning

**What this file is for:** the four or five sentences that describe you, so you are not interchangeable with the next designer a recruiter scrolls past.

**Why Claude needs it:** when you ask Claude to write a bio, a resume profile, or a cover note, this file is the source it pulls from. Without it, Claude writes a generic designer.

**Class:** Class 7. You fill it in once in class, then revisit every three months.

Here is the problem this file solves. A recruiter has two tabs open. Yours, and another designer in Dhaka with the same years of experience and roughly the same Figma skills. If both profiles say "passionate UX designer with an eye for detail," the recruiter picks whichever loaded first. Positioning is how you stop being the second tab.

Your first version will be rough. Write it anyway. A rough specific answer beats a polished vague one.

---

## The EduBridge example

This is the example, filled in by a designer who finished the EduBridge booking screen in Class 6. It is here so you can see the shape. Do not copy it.

### One-sentence positioning

Format: "I am a [role] who [specific verb] [specific kind of work] for [specific market or audience]."

Version A: "I am a UX designer who builds trust-first mobile flows for first-time online-payment users in Bangladesh."

Version B: "I am a product designer who turns vague client briefs into shippable mobile flows for BD startups."

Version C: "I am a designer who works in the gap between an offshore client's brief and what the local market actually needs."

Picked: version A. It names a market, a user problem (trust), and a device reality. A recruiter can repeat it to a colleague a day later.

### What I am good at, three things, each with evidence

1. Interrogating a brief. I find what is missing before anything gets drawn. Evidence: `projects/edubridge/brief-v3-interrogated.md`, where eleven assumptions in the client brief got named and four got overturned.
2. Designing for low-end Android. I default to a sub-15K taka device on 3G, not a laptop window narrowed to 400px. Evidence: the booking screen keeps the photo below the fold and holds all motion under 150ms.
3. Directing Claude instead of prompting it. I lock the brief and the tokens first, then let Claude write the markup. Evidence: `projects/edubridge/tokens.md` was finished before `booking-screen.html` existed.

### What I am not good at yet, three things

1. Brand identity from scratch. I am a product designer, not a brand designer.
2. Marketing motion. I do functional motion. Hero animations are not my craft.
3. Native iOS and Android patterns. I design for mobile web and rely on engineers to translate.

Naming these makes you read as more senior, not less. It also tells you what to learn next.

### Where I want to be in 18 months

"Senior enough to own a whole product area at a BD company with real users, not a title bump. The path: three more projects at EduBridge scale where I own the brief, a portfolio that shows the whole arc from broken brief to shipped screen, and enough visible writing that recruiters open the conversation instead of me."

Specific beats ambitious. "Director at a big tech company" is not a plan. "Owning a product area at a fifty-person BD fintech" is.

### Elevator pitch, thirty seconds

"I'm a product designer. I work on apps for people in Bangladesh using entry-level Android phones, mostly money or education products. Most of my job is figuring out the real problem before any design starts, because the brief almost never has it written down."

Say it out loud. If it does not sound like something you would actually say to a person at a wedding, rewrite it.

### The short answer when someone says AI did your work

"AI made the execution fast. The decisions are mine. I decided the brief was wrong about who the user was. I decided parents, not students. I dropped the video call feature because the budget could not carry it. Those calls are what you are hiring. The HTML is the easy part."

This is the casual version, for a meetup or a comment thread. The full interview version lives in `04-interview-answers.md`.
